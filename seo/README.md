# rizzroulette.chat — static SEO layer

Built 30 July 2026, against the findings in `seo-audit-rizzroulette-chat-2026-07-30`.

This fixes the **Critical** tier of that audit: 17 server-rendered pages, complete metadata,
JSON-LD, `robots.txt` and `sitemap.xml`. Every byte is in the initial HTML response — no
client-side rendering anywhere in this layer, which is the whole point.

difference — a real LCP problem on the mobile traffic this vertical runs on. Schema still points at
the full-size file.

headline is the one genuinely off-brand element in this bundle. Re-run `make-og.py` on a machine
with Bebas Neue installed, or rebuild the card in Canva, and it'll be exact. Everything else matches.

broken internal link:

`/chat` · `/creators/apply` · `/terms` · `/privacy` · `/2257` · `/dmca`

**2. Serve `site/` as static files** at the domain root, ahead of your SPA. Directory-style URLs

## Before you publish — four things I deliberately did not do for you

**1. Competitor claims are vague on purpose.** Every `theirs` value in `competitors.js` reads
"Paid tier" / "Not published" / "Varies". Comparative claims about a named business are a legal
exposure, not just an SEO choice. Open each competitor's live pricing page, and either replace the
value with something you can screenshot and date, or leave it vague. Don't invent specifics.

**2. No fabricated social proof.** I wrote no testimonials, no star ratings, no review counts, no
"1,247 users online". Your competitors do all four. Note specifically: **do not add
`AggregateRating` or `Review` schema without real, verifiable reviews** — fake review markup is an
explicit Google structured-data violation and earns a manual action, which is far worse than the
invisibility you have now. `/about` says plainly that you don't do this. That's a positioning
asset; don't undercut it.

**3. `/about` has an unfilled TODO.** Named operators with roles are the strongest E-E-A-T signal
available and their absence was a documented audit finding. If you'd rather not publish full names
for an adult platform — legitimate — publish roles and first names at minimum.

**4. "Industry-best" is a superlative, and superlatives need evidence.** This is the one change I
want to flag rather than just ship. "Best gender ratio in random video chat" is a comparative
advertising claim about every named competitor at once. Under FTC guidance in the US and ASA rules
in the UK/EU, the advertiser has to be able to substantiate it — and a competitor or a regulator can
ask you to. Nobody publishes their ratio, so you cannot prove you beat them, and they cannot prove
you don't; that ambiguity cuts both ways and is usually why these claims survive. Three ways to
lower the exposure, in order of how much punch they cost you:

- **Keep it, and build the evidence.** Log your own hourly ratio and publish it on
  `/how-it-works`. A live or monthly figure turns an unprovable superlative into a documented one,
  and it is a genuinely rare trust signal in this category. This is the strongest option.
- **Attribute it.** "Built for the best gender ratio in random video chat" or "designed around"
  — describes intent rather than asserting a measured fact.
- **Swap the axis.** "The only random video chat that pays creators to keep the queue balanced" is
  fully provable, still differentiating, and nobody can contest it.

All three are one edit: `BRAND.ratioClaim`, `ratioClaimShort` and `ratioTableValue` in
`build/theme.js`. Your call — I've shipped what you asked for.

**5. Verify your own claims before shipping.** I wrote copy asserting automated age estimation with
document fallback, 80/20 creator split, device fingerprinting, and a published appeal route. Those
came from your description of the product. The audit noted your age gate currently returns a binary
Minor/adult label with no fallback path in the UI. **If the fallback isn't live, that sentence is
false advertising** — either ship the fallback or soften the copy. Same test for every other claim.

## What this does not fix

Being direct about the boundary, because the gap matters:

- **Links.** You have roughly none, and this is what actually decides rankings. No file can
  generate referring domains. Tube-site placements, paid directory listings, exchanges with
  non-competing adult properties, ad-network partner relationships. Budget it as a real line item.
- **Listicle inclusion.** Getting into the "best random video chat alternatives" round-ups that
  already rank is your only realistic route into AI-engine citations, since Perplexity and ChatGPT
  won't recommend adult platforms from your own domain. That's outreach, not code.
- **Translations.** `hreflang` tags are written but commented out. Uncomment per locale **only as
  each translation actually ships** — pointing hreflang at a non-existent page is worse than
  omitting it. Portuguese and Spanish first, given your creator pipeline.
- **The disposable-email classification.** A third party lists `rizzroulette.chat` as a forwarding
  email domain. Submit a correction, and consider a separate sending domain for transactional mail
  so the two reputations aren't shared.
- **Core Web Vitals.** Run `pagespeed.web.dev` after deploy. These pages are single-file with
  inline CSS so they should score well; the one blocking request is Google Fonts. If it hurts,
  self-host the three fonts and drop the `<link>`.
